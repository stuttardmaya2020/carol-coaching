# Product: Carol Fox Coaching website

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Product purpose

A small website for Carol Fox, a London-based life and business coach and former headhunter. Its job is to turn a warm referral into an enquiry. Most visitors have been sent by someone who knows Carol, so the site doesn't need to find clients. It needs to make them feel confident enough to get in touch.

The single action is an informal first chat: a coffee or a call, paid for by Carol, with no obligation. There is no online booking, payment or package to buy.

## Users

Referred visitors at every career stage, usually arriving from a link shared on a phone.

| Who | Arrives via | Their worry | What wins them over |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The starter: a recent graduate entering their first job | A parent who was Carol's client | "Coaching is for executives, and I can't afford it" | A friendly tone, people their age, a free first chat |
| The stuck professional: mid-career, wants to work better and enjoy it more | A colleague or friend | "Will this turn into a long, expensive programme?" | Proof that one session may be enough, testimonials from peers |
| The leader: founder or C-suite | A peer or network contact | "Is she senior enough to challenge me?" | Her headhunting background, recognition from Kathleen Saxton, an incisive voice |
| The referrer: a past client | Their own coaching with Carol | "Will this reflect well on me if I send it?" | A polished, modern site that's easy to share |

Within a minute, a visitor should think: "She gets people like me, she's credible, and talking to her costs me nothing."

## Positioning

Confirmed against the comparable site review (`research/comparable-sites/`). Three things a neighbouring coach could not truthfully copy:

1. **One coach for every career stage.** Carol coaches graduates through to CEOs, and life as well as work. Competitors narrow to a single audience.
2. **A former headhunter's eye.** She has spent a career judging people, roles and potential, which positions her quickly with senior visitors.
3. **A first step that is a coffee on her.** "A coffee or a call, on me. No obligation." This is warmer than any competitor's chemistry call, consultation or paid trial.

## Operating context

- Visitors arrive by referral, usually from a link shared on a phone, often after a personal recommendation.
- The enquiry is a mailto link or phone number, or at most a placeholder contact form. Carol replies personally and arranges the coffee or call herself.
- Carol coaches in person in London and remotely.
- The site is first previewed on GitHub Pages, then moved to Carol's own domain once she approves it.
- The redesign is also Maya's portfolio case study, so decisions are logged in `design-log.md`.

## Capabilities and constraints

- The site can only start a conversation. No booking, payment, accounts or client area.
- Undecided until Carol confirms, so don't state them: session prices, packages, session length and format beyond the first chat. Describe how sessions work loosely, and don't invent figures.
- Contact details are placeholders until Carol confirms them.

## Brand and tone

Carol describes her style as "supportive, incisive and quick" (and on her old site "incisive, supportive, challenging and fast"). The site should feel warm, experienced and unhurried, with no pressure, jargon or hard sell. Write in UK English, in Carol's first-person voice.

## Content

- Pages: Home (the whole story on one scroll), About Carol, How I work, Get in touch. Every page ends with an invitation to talk.
- 12 existing testimonials from graduates to CEOs (see `research/current-site/screenshots/testimonials.png`). Use 2 or 3 per page, matched to the visitor.
- Credentials: 20+ years coaching, 3,000+ people, former headhunter, NLP practitioner, counselling and psychotherapy training, Emergenetics profiling.
- Recognition: Kathleen Saxton acknowledged Carol in her book on leadership and toxic leadership as a leader she enjoyed working with, and mentioned her on a networking panel. Never word this so it suggests Carol is a toxic leader.
- Contact details are placeholders: `hello@example.com`, `07700 900000`. No office address. Say Carol coaches in London and remotely.
- Don't use the old home page photo with Jane. Photos with Alfie, Carol's late Dalmatian, are optional, and the copy must not describe him as current.

## Evidence on hand

- **Testimonials:** 12, from graduates to CEOs, which may be quoted with the names and roles shown on the old site. The only source is the screenshot `research/current-site/screenshots/testimonials.png`, so transcribe them exactly. Don't write new ones.
- **Carol's own words:** "incisive, supportive, challenging and fast" and "no strings attached and on me!" (old site screenshots in `research/current-site/screenshots/`).
- **Credentials and recognition:** as listed under Content. The Kathleen Saxton book title isn't confirmed, so refer to the book without naming it.
- **Photos:** Carol's black-and-white portraits exist only inside screenshots of the old site. The original files aren't in the repo yet, so use clearly marked placeholders. Don't crop the screenshots, and don't use stock images in Carol's place.
- **Absent, so never fabricate:** client logos, case studies, statistics beyond the credentials above, press coverage, prices and the book title.

## Product principles

1. **The coffee is the product.** Every page should make the free, no-obligation first chat obvious and easy to take up.
2. **Every visitor sees themselves.** A graduate and a CEO should both feel the site is for them. Don't let one career stage take over.
3. **Credibility without pressure.** Prove seniority through her background, real testimonials and recognition, never through superlatives or a hard sell.
4. **Safe to share.** A past client should be glad to send the link. Accuracy and polish protect the referrer as well as Carol.
5. **Only what's true.** Use real words, real people and confirmed facts. Mark anything unconfirmed as a placeholder.

## Accessibility and inclusion

- WCAG 2.2 AA as a minimum. Many visitors are older (the referrers and senior leaders), so keep body text at 17px or larger, with strong contrast and generous tap targets.
- Imagery should show people of different ages and backgrounds.
- Works well on a phone, since most referrals arrive as a shared link.
